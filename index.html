How it works

  • Mass balance. Soil increase = rock content × cumulative rock ÷ soil mass (depth × bulk density). A limit binds when starting level + increase ≥ limit. All applied rock counts, dissolved or not; no leaching or crop uptake (conservative).
  • Rock chemistry. Basalt: Gard 2019, basalt–andesite (45–63 wt% SiO₂), kriged per 0.1° source cell (Ni, Cr, Co from the Mg relationship; As, Cd class medians). Ultramafics: Gard rock-type medians; As and Cd for ultramafics are literature-range placeholders.
  • Pairing. Each field is paired with its nearest basalt source (or the most PTE-rich within 300 km), a proxy for routing.
  • Limits. Noema's regulatory database, one screening-tier value per country. Where limits depend on soil conditions they are matched to each field: China and Portugal by soil pH, Slovakia and the Czech Republic by texture (SoilGrids). China's two middle pH classes are filled from GB 15618-2018 and still need checking against the standard. EU Directive or the global median of national limits where a country has none.
  • Background. One global set from Kabata-Pendias (2001), not spatial: world means, except Cd, which uses the US agricultural geometric mean (0.175 mg/kg) because the world arithmetic mean (0.53) is inflated by contaminated soils. Other values agree with national surveys within about ±30%; Cr is the most sensitive (its background is about half its limit). Hou et al. (2025) exceedance probabilities are used only to exclude likely-contaminated fields; they cannot be converted to concentrations.
  • Not modelled. Real routing, per-field dissolution with pH feedback (approximated by the decline factor), plant uptake, leaching, Cr speciation, pasture land. Legal limits are not toxicity thresholds.
